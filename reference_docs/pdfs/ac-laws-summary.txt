# Association Croquet Laws Summary for Simulator
# Extracted from AC Laws 7th Edition - Key rules for gameplay implementation

================================================================================
BASIC GAME STRUCTURE
================================================================================

OBJECTIVE:
- Each side has two balls (Blue/Black vs Red/Yellow in singles, or partners)
- Run all 12 hoops in order with both balls, then hit the peg with both
- First to peg out both balls wins (26 points total per side)

HOOP ORDER:
1. Hoop 1 (front of court, right side facing south)
2. Hoop 2 (front, left side)
3. Hoop 3 (middle, right side)
4. Hoop 4 (middle, left side)
5. Hoop 5 (back, right side)
6. Hoop 6 (back, left side)
7. 1-back (hoop 1 from opposite direction)
8. 2-back (hoop 2 from opposite direction)
9. 3-back (hoop 3 from opposite direction)
10. 4-back (hoop 4 from opposite direction)
11. Penultimate/Penult (hoop 5 from opposite direction)
12. Rover (hoop 6 from opposite direction)
13. Peg out

COURT DIMENSIONS:
- Full size: 35 yards x 28 yards
- Yard line: 1 yard inside boundary
- Baulk lines: 1 yard inside yard line on short boundaries

================================================================================
TURN STRUCTURE
================================================================================

BASIC TURN:
- Strike your ball once with the mallet
- Turn ends unless you score a hoop or make a roquet

ROQUET:
- Hitting another ball with the striker's ball
- Can roquet each of the other 3 balls once per turn
- After running a hoop, roquet rights are restored (can hit all 4 again)

CROQUET STROKE:
- After a roquet, place striker's ball in contact with roqueted ball
- Strike so both balls move (or would move but for obstacles)
- After croquet stroke, take a continuation stroke

FOUR-BALL BREAK:
- Ideal break uses all 4 balls as pioneers and pivot
- Pioneer: ball placed near next hoop
- Pivot: ball kept near middle of court
- Escape ball: ball used after running hoop to get to pioneer

================================================================================
HOOP RUNNING
================================================================================

SCORING A HOOP POINT:
- Ball must start outside hoop (no part through)
- Ball must end having passed through (at least partially)
- Direction must be correct for that hoop
- Ball can score in multiple strokes if legally positioned

HOOP POSITION:
- "Through" = any part of ball past non-playing side
- "Clear" = whole ball past non-playing side
- Ball in jaws can complete in later stroke

================================================================================
FAULTS (INVALID STROKES)
================================================================================

STRIKING FAULTS:
- Double tap: hitting striker's ball twice in one stroke
- Push: maintaining contact too long (not a clean hit)
- Crush: pressing ball against hoop or peg
- Hitting wrong ball
- Striking with wrong end of mallet

CROQUET STROKE FAULTS:
- Croqueted ball doesn't move
- Striker's ball not in contact at start
- Mallet contacts croqueted ball directly

CONSEQUENCE OF FAULT:
- Turn ends immediately
- Balls may be replaced or left at opponent's choice (depending on fault type)

================================================================================
BALL IN HAND / BALL POSITIONS
================================================================================

YARD LINE RULE:
- Ball ending within yard of boundary is placed on yard line
- Exception: ball at rest after running hoop stays where it is
- Balls replaced on yard line: mark spot opposite where ball crossed

WIRING:
- A ball is "wired" from another if a hoop/peg prevents direct shot
- Matters for lift entitlements

CORNER SPOTS:
- 1 yard from each boundary
- Used for specific placements

================================================================================
LIFTS AND CONTACT
================================================================================

LIFT:
- Opponent may lift to either baulk line if:
  - Striker leaves balls wired from each other, OR
  - Striker's ball ends in specific positions (after running 1-back or 4-back)

ADVANCED PLAY RULE:
- After running 1-back or 4-back, opponent gets lift option
- Designed to balance advantage of making a break

SUPER-ADVANCED PLAY:
- Additional options including "contact" (place ball touching enemy ball)
- Applied at 1-back and 4-back completion

================================================================================
PEELING
================================================================================

PEEL:
- Sending a ball other than striker's ball through its hoop
- Does NOT give you extra strokes (opponent's ball)
- DOES score the point for that ball

TRIPLE PEEL:
- Peeling partner ball through 4-back, penult, and rover in one turn
- Considered skilled play

STRAIGHT PEEL:
- Peel from directly behind hoop
- Usually done in same stroke as running hoop yourself

================================================================================
END GAME
================================================================================

ROVER BALL:
- Ball that has run all 12 hoops
- Can roquet and be roqueted normally
- Can be pegged out by any ball

PEGGING OUT:
- Hit rover ball onto peg
- Ball is removed from game
- Cannot peg out partner ball if it would leave opponent with a rover vs non-rover

WINNING:
- First side to peg out both balls wins
- Score is difference in points (max 26-0)

================================================================================
COMMON TACTICAL RULES
================================================================================

TAKING CROQUET FROM A BALL:
- Must take croquet where balls lie (with adjustment for yard line)
- Various stroke types: drive, stop-shot, roll, pass-roll, split

RUSH:
- Roquet that sends roqueted ball to a useful position
- "Cut rush" = at angle
- "Straight rush" = direct line

LEAVE:
- Ball positions left at end of turn
- "New Standard Leave" (NSL) = common defensive arrangement
- Goal: make opponent's next turn difficult

BREAK DOWN:
- Failing to maintain the break
- Usually from poor position or missed roquet
- Must assess risk of continuing vs playing safe

================================================================================
SIMULATOR-SPECIFIC NOTES
================================================================================

SHOT DIFFICULTY FACTORS:
- Distance (roquets harder at range)
- Angle (cut rushes harder than straight)
- Hoop approach angle matters
- Jump shots and hampered strokes are very difficult

AI DECISION POINTS:
- Shoot at ball or lay up defensively?
- Which ball to roquet first?
- Where to place pioneers?
- When to attempt peels?
- Risk assessment: aggressive break vs safe leave

BALL STATE TRACKING:
- For each ball track: current hoop, position, whether it's a rover
- For turn track: balls roqueted this turn, whether hoop run since last roquet
- Reset roquet rights after each hoop
